november was an active month, relatively speaking. my solar return is coming up and marks my second birthday out of the city. whatever beliefs ppl have about the rural midwest, i've had my fair share of exposure to what bestie describes as "[my] kind of strange." where is the beauty! apparently it's somewhere near [the cross at the crossroads](https://crossusa.org/).

## weird objects

my sister picked up some artwork in stl for an upcoming show at her museum, so i tagged along as entertainment for the 2 hour drive. we checked out the pulitzer arts foundation since they're hosting work by [olga de amaral](https://olgadeamaral.art/), a colombian fiber artist. 

what i realized belatedly while editing my photos + reading up on amaral + writing this post is that my initial response was maybe, probably, reactionary. i went into the exhibition blind with little knowledge about textiles and no knowledge about amaral, let alone experimental/abstract fabric art. amaral's work is appropriately bizarre, and cool as a result.

her work shares less with decorative tapestry (i.e., what i imagine when i hear `textile artist`, and what i expected walking in) and more in common with structures, sculptures, built environments. i appreciated how one large object repeated many smaller forms, evoking tactility when we could see but not touch. (the tapestry made up of blue-tipped ropes reminded me of octavia butler's oankali.)

{{< photo-grid caption="Me + a tapestry, maybe, courtesy sheesh + what i am unofficially calling the gold gallery @ the Amaral exhibition" >}}

{{< /photo-grid >}}

i'm used to viewing tapestries hung on a wall, but pulitzer curated most galleries (maybe all?) with at least one structure hanging from the ceiling. that let me + other museum-goers do a little dance with amaral's work. we can't touch, but we can move through space as we look, perceive changes in light + shadow (ergo experience repetition's effect on depth + space), and watch how each installation changes in relation to our point-of-view. looking turns into studying: how did she do that???

{{< photo-grid caption="Sheesh next to el gran muro for scale (+i'm bummed i didn't get a better picture!) + photo of el gran muro @ Peachtree, courtesy of SmartHistory" >}}
amaral-el-gran-muro-sheesh.webp
peachtree-gran-amaral.webp
{{< /photo-grid >}}

this one is [_el gran muro_ (1976)](https://smarthistory.org/olga-de-amaral-el-gran-muro/), a commission for the peachtree hotel in atlanta. this isn't the entirety of el gran muro, which spans about six stories high, so what you're looking at is "only" some sections of the installation. the gallery attendant told us each section weighed several hundred pounds and was the most difficult install for the exhibition. my sister drilled the attendant about insurance policies (they're expensive), whether it was shipped in a freight (yes), and how they actually did the install (don't remember the answer to this).

 anyway, i'm getting a D- in museum-goer because i forgot to note the name/material of each work. the pulitzer [wrote a guide](https://issuu.com/pulitzerarts/docs/fall_2026_exhibition_guide_olga_de_b6974811d4b584?fr=sNGI0NjkyODE2OTQ) to accompany their exhibition, overviewing amaral's background as an architect and her different eras working with experimental form + material. the guide doesn't pair pictures with their write-up, so scroll through their [(limited) gallery](https://pulitzerarts.org/art/olga-de-amaral-weaving-the-infinite/), too.

{{< photo src="pulitzer-tadao-ando.webp" caption="Water Court @ Pulitzer Arts Foundation, St Louis, MO, designed by Tadao Ando" />}}

another neat thing is the pulitzer building was designed by a japanese architect, [tadao ando](https://www.instagram.com/tadao_ando_ando/?hl=en). i usually love concrete, but i'm not sure my immediate reaction was "love" while i walked around the building. it felt cold and sterile, and not a place i'd want to hang out at all day. next time we go in i'll have to pay more attention and read up on [Ando's approach to design](https://artic.contentdm.oclc.org/digital/collection/caohp/id/40). i wonder how amaral felt about concrete...

i plan on doing a longer post about me + drumming + losing interest in the kit in lieu of percussion + karen carpenter + whatever. this month, though, i also made it out to the student percussion ensemble where the music department introduced me to [experimental percussion](https://www.thetonearm.com/the-solitary-ensemble-of-erik-hall-minimalist-trilogy-solo-three/), i heard [batá performed live](https://folklife.si.edu/magazine/gender-taboos-cuban-bata-drumming) (my drum instructor performed ♡♡♡), and i realized that i really love percussion. 

i want to play timpani and glockenspiel, and steal someone's [hammered dulcimer](https://youtu.be/QN3KWbpBsvg?si=RyaTU0v77ZGm7vr_). one of my 2027 resolutions is to learn orchestral grip since my priority this year was learning classic snare and trad grip. 

## reading + journal

last time we went to st louis we tried out a new-to-us indie bookstore, leftbank books. it's definitely a favorite right now, with a solid selection of contemporary literary fiction + lit in translation. their scifi section is so/so. it has the classics, but it's no bucket-o-blood or family bookstore. still, i love that place and so does sheesh, so we went after pulitzer.

i resolved to read more in 2026 like i have every year. the first part of the year i spent most of this year reading on my kobo, but now that reading is established firmly in my routine i've started reading/collecting physical books again. i've read _buffalo hunter hunter_ already and i've started _morel's invention_. brief pause on _morel_ because i'm making my way through _i saw ramallah_, a library book.

working through a reading journal has also been nice. i spend extra time with whatever i've just read, chewing on it a bit more. i've almost finished the journal i've been using since july and have my 2027 one lined up with some plans to do spreads + add some structure to my stream-of-conscious entries. lady whiskers hates when i'm focusing on my journal(s). ♡
