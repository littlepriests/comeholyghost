weird objects

my sister picked up some artwork in st l for an upcoming show at her museum, so i tagged along as entertainment for the two hour drive. we checked out the pulitzer arts foundation since they're hosting a collection by olga de amaral , a colombian fiber artist.

what i realized belatedly while editing my photos + reading up on amaral + writing this post is that my initial response was maybe, probably, reactionary. i went into the exhibition blind with little knowledge about textiles and no knowledge about amaral, let alone experimental/abstract fabric art. still, amaral's work is often bizarre, but the variety holds your attention long enough to get you up to speed.

olga de amaral @ pulitzer arts foundation (_weaving the infinite_, st louis, MO, sep 10, 2026–jan 31, 2027)

the scale of her textiles surprised me, both in size and how they contorted in space. amaral uses repeated, smaller forms that unify into a much larger product. but the actual material she weaves offsets that unity, gives certain textiles a wildness that holds your eye. i think, too, the density of a messy material evokes tactility, which seems necessary when an object meant to be felt can only be seen but not touched.

(the tapestry made up of blue-tipped ropes reminded me of octavia butler's oankali.)

me + maybe a tapestry courtesy sheesh + what i am unofficially calling the gold gallery @ the Amaral exhibition

i didn't expect amaral's work to share more with structure, sculpture, and built environments than, say, renaissance tapestries. to accommodate how amaral's objects occupy space, pulitzer curated most galleries (maybe all?) with at least one structure, often several, suspended away from the walls. that let me + other museum-goers do a little dance with amaral's work.

we can't touch, but we can move through space as we look, perceive changes in light + shadow, experience how repetition affects depth + space, and watch how each installation changes in relation to our point-of-view. looking is an effortless study; the breadth of detail feels endless.

sheesh next to el gran muro for scale (+i'm bummed i didn't get a better picture!) + photo of el gran muro @ peachtree, courtesy of smarthistory

_el gran muro_ (1976) was a commission for the peachtree hotel in atlanta, and spans about six stories. the gallery attendant told us each section weighed several hundred pounds and was the most difficult install for the exhibition. my sister grilled the attendant about insurance policies (they're expensive), whether it was shipped in a freight (yes), and the process the museum used to handle the install (don't remember the answer to this).
anyway, i'm getting a D- in museum-goer because i forgot to note the name/material of each work. the pulitzer wrote a guide to accompany their exhibition, overviewing amaral's background as an architect and her different eras working with experimental form + material. the guide doesn't pair pictures with their write-up, so scroll through their (limited) digital gallery , too.

water court @ pulitzer arts foundation, st louis, MO, designed by tadao ando

another neat thing. japanese architect tadao ando designed the pulitzer building. i usually love concrete, but i'm not sure my immediate reaction was "love" while i walked from room to room. the concrete appeared soft, supple, but i still managed to feel cold and sterile inside it. not a place i'd want to hang out at all day. next time we go in i'll have to pay more attention and read up on ando's approach to design . would i feel differently on a sunnier day? i wonder how amaral felt about concrete...

33.9 single (f) seeking hammered dulcimer

percussion ensemble @ the university

i plan on doing a longer post about me + drumming + losing interest in the kit in lieu of percussion + karen carpenter. for the sake of today, i'll summarize.

i made it out to the student percussion ensemble and i hope to make it a habit. the music department introduced me to experimental percussion , i heard batá performed live (my drum instructor performed ♡♡♡), and i reaffirmed my love for all these odd instruments.
one of my 2027 resolutions is to learn orchestral grip since i've been prioritizing snare and trad grip. i want to play timpani and glockenspiel, and steal someone's hammered dulcimer . ultimately, really, i need to develop strong opinions about mallets.

reading + journal

last time we went to stl we tried out a new-to-us indie bookstore, leftbank books. it's definitely a favorite right now, with a solid selection of contemporary literary fiction + lit in translation. it was a nice end to our fun museum day.

leftbank, my october/november book haul, lady whiskers hating AM writing time. ♡♡

i've read _buffalo hunter hunter_ already and i've started _morel's invention_. brief pause on _morel_ because i'm making my way through _i saw ramallah_, a library book.

working through a reading journal has also been nice. i spend extra time with whatever i've just read, lets me chew on it a bit more. this is great when a read was truly excellent (_our share of night_ by mariana enríquez) but sucks when the read was a drag (galápagos by vonnegut; sry). i might do a wrap up based on my reading journal and revisit / do a review of some faves from the year.

i've almost finished the journal i started last july and have my 2027 one lined up. i have plans for spreads and i'm stoked to add some structure to my stream-of-conscious entries.

✝ @ the crossroads

reminds me of the i4 eyesore, two buildings made for each other. love is love ♡

i don't really have much to say about the ✝. it looks like two washington monuments slapped together? it's so tacky that it makes the long drive worth it? might be worth rabbit holing over with the i4 eyesore... the double meaning of the name shows a real joie de vivre at least, so uncharacteristic of our evangelical community members...

anyway november was a relatively active month for a homebody like me. my solar return is coming up and marks my second birthday out of the city. i have a fair bit of travel coming up: sac, a weekend stl trip, maybe an overnight in chicago, and then florida.

til next time~
